# Preliminary Work and Prototype Development

We have developed an initial AI-assisted teaching framework and integrated existing course materials into a traceable reference library to support the proposed faculty co-design and curriculum adaptation research. This preliminary work provides a concrete starting point for developing the EOP-specific platform: an organized collection of instructional resources, a workflow for adapting those resources, and mechanisms for tracing selected content to its source. The current prototype focuses on air quality, connecting pollutant sources, atmospheric transport, environmental and health effects, measurement, and modeling. These resources provide a domain foundation for the broader systems representation that participating faculty will develop during the project.

The prototype organizes curriculum development into five functional roles: preparing topic cards from source materials, identifying instructional needs and planning lessons, developing assessments, assembling instructional materials, and reviewing the resulting content. These roles are implemented as reusable instructions for an AI assistant, supported by shared templates and reference resources. Topic cards organize concepts, prerequisites, examples, assumptions, and potential EOP connections. The workflow specifies that instructional objectives should guide the selection of activities and assessments, and that faculty should review proposed lesson plans before materials are generated. This structure makes the decisions involved in adaptation explicit and provides identifiable points at which faculty can contribute disciplinary knowledge, correct content, and refine instructional choices. It is an initial framework for continued development, rather than a completed classroom platform.

To establish the reference collection, we incorporated 38 instructional PDFs comprising 1,050 pages from three courses taught by the PI: an undergraduate course in air pollution and hazardous waste control, a graduate course in smart air quality monitoring and air pollution control, and a graduate course in air pollution modeling. The supplied materials span shared air-pollution foundations, measurement and sensor technologies, and computational modeling. Each document is retained in its original form and accompanied by a page-organized Markdown reference, source identifiers, and page images. Course and topic indexes support navigation across the collection, while links identify the original document and PDF page. Retaining visual references is particularly important for these materials because equations, instrument diagrams, maps, and tables are not fully represented by text extraction alone.

The collection also provides concrete examples of overlap and complementary coverage across courses. Gaussian dispersion concepts appear in both the undergraduate and graduate modeling materials; the monitoring course adds content on instrument selection, calibration, and measurement limitations. The library retains these separate course contexts rather than treating all material on a shared topic as interchangeable. Selected passages are linked to eight existing topic cards. This organization provides a basis for investigating how faculty can draw on shared content while preserving the technical scope and instructional context needed for their own courses. The supplied courses serve as initial reference resources; their inclusion does not represent completed course redesign or multi-instructor co-design.

Development checks confirmed coverage of all source documents and pages, preservation of the original PDF files, and the integrity of internal source links. Selected retrieval checks located Gaussian-model assumptions and a worked example, sensor calibration and cross-sensitivity discussions, and roadway-model receptor-placement guidance, each with an identifiable source and page. Inspection of representative pages also confirmed the need to consult original visuals for equation- and figure-dependent content. These checks support the technical feasibility of organizing and retrieving traceable instructional references. They do not establish the accuracy of all imported content or the quality of subsequently generated teaching materials.

The proposed project will build on this foundation by engaging faculty at UCF and Valencia College to contribute instructional materials, review and develop topic cards, refine the framework, and use it to adapt their courses. The planned comparison of co-design only, co-design plus AI, and AI-only support will examine questions that the prototype has not yet addressed empirically: faculty effort, the depth and coherence of EOP integration, persistence of instructional changes, and student systems thinking.
