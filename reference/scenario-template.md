# Teaching Scenario Template

Use for a new case or a substantive revision. The assistant can fill this outline from an ordinary
conversation; instructors need not edit structured metadata. Preserve the existing formats and IDs
of older cases. This template does not require retrospective reformatting or imply completed review.
Use the [shared policy](workflow-policy.md) and [review rubric](review-rubric.md).

## Identity and contributions

- Stable scenario ID, title, revision, and revision date.
- Case type: hypothetical, real setting with hypothetical exercises, or evidence-based case;
  explain any mixture rather than using the label as proof of validity.
- Original author/source, contributors, adapters, and AI assistance, where known.
- Case recommendation, source provision, and review contributions, separately attributed.
- Coverage: partial or full for the stated scope. Author acceptance: unconfirmed or supported by
  an actual response for this revision. Review: not reviewed or linked to the actual review record.
- Source and asset permissions, including unknown or restricted reuse.

## Teaching question and scope

State the decision or question, relevant setting and alternatives, and what students could explain,
analyze, or evaluate. Specify geographic, temporal, and system boundaries. State what the case cannot
answer. A regional location is not evidence that a hypothetical project actually exists.

## Systems relationships and topic cards

Identify the relationships the case can help students examine and link relevant cards. For example,
travel activity may affect emissions, while concentration and exposure require additional evidence.
Distinguish a source-supported relationship from an instructional hypothesis or simplification.
Identify feedback only when an actual mechanism is described. Record candidate EOP connections with
observable student reasoning, not a fixed list of outcomes or an attainment claim.

## Evidence and input provenance

| Claim or input | Source and locator | Evidence type | Applicability and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replace with an actual entry | File/page, or URL/date/section | Observation, forecast, stipulated input, or derived value | Units, period, boundary, missing evidence |

Use existing source and claim IDs; register new teaching-relevant factual claims in the
[fact register](fact-register.md). Record what sources are included and what must be obtained elsewhere.
Separate report forecasts from hypothetical exercise values. Record computation checks and their
limits separately from verification of input data. Never fill absent real observations with unlabeled
synthetic values or attribute a case recommendation to someone without evidence.

## Teaching options and practical requirements

Describe possible interpretation, calculation, or modeling activities where supported. State student
preparation, skills that can be taught, time, tools, accessible resources, and data readiness for each.
A scenario is not a finished assessment: Intake selects the scope and Assessment Designer develops
tasks and scoring after plan review and acceptance. Keep any example solutions in instructor resources.
Say when this case is a poor fit and allow another context or no scenario.

## Gaps and revision record

List missing evidence, conflicts, unfinished examples, and the uses each gap prevents. Record review
findings, contributor corrections, and affected downstream materials. A partial case may support
source interpretation while being unsuitable for a quantitative prediction. Do not mark it generally
ready simply because its documentation is complete.

When adding the case, update the [catalog](../scenario/catalog.md) with its teaching applications,
prerequisites/support, data readiness/limits, and revision. When revising a used case, apply the shared
policy's rules for affected plans, assessments, and exports; preserve unrelated accepted decisions.
