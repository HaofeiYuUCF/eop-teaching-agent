# EOP Framework Learning Outcomes

Derived from The Lemelson Foundation (2026). *The Engineering for One Planet Framework:
Foundational Learning Outcomes to Prepare Engineers to Protect and Improve Earth and the Life it
Sustains (2026).* Cynthia Anderson and Cindy Cooper (Eds). Portland, Oregon, USA. CC BY-NC-SA.

94 outcomes: 47 core, 47 supplemental, across nine topic areas.

## How to read an entry

`ID | Bloom | ABET: n | SDG: n`

- **ID** — topic abbreviation, C for core or S for supplemental, and number. `ST-C1` is Systems
  Thinking core outcome 1.
- **Bloom** — the framework collapses Bloom's six levels into three. Low covers remember and
  understand. Medium covers apply and analyze. High covers evaluate and create. The level shown
  is the catalog's published-level representation. Record any adapted objective separately and
  justify its actual cognitive demand from the task and evidence, rather than its verb alone.
- **ABET** — the ABET Criterion 3 student outcomes this aligns with (see below).
- **SDG** — the UN Sustainable Development Goals this relates to.

Core and supplemental do not correlate with Bloom level. Core means most essential. Supplemental
means beyond what the framework's collaborators deemed essential.

## ABET Criterion 3 student outcomes

1. Identify, formulate, and solve complex engineering problems
2. Apply engineering design to produce solutions meeting needs, with consideration of public
   health, safety, welfare, and global, cultural, social, environmental, and economic factors
3. Communicate effectively with a range of audiences
4. Recognize ethical and professional responsibilities and make informed judgments considering
   global, economic, environmental, and societal impacts
5. Function effectively on a team
6. Develop and conduct experimentation, analyze and interpret data, draw conclusions
7. Acquire and apply new knowledge using appropriate learning strategies

# Systems Thinking

Central to and interconnected with every other outcome in the framework. Contributors identified
systems thinking from an environmental and social perspective as the most fundamental approach
students must learn.

## Core

**ST-C1** | Low | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 4, 11-13, 17
Explain interconnectedness (e.g., intersecting, related, and/or connected systems; human actions,
infrastructure, and global environmental, climate, and social impacts, risks, future impacts, and
consequences; synergies and rebound effects) and how all human-made designs and activities rely
upon and are embedded within ecological, physical, climate, and social systems.

**ST-C2** | Medium | ABET: 1, 2 | SDG: 9, 11-13, 17
Analyze dynamic impacts and interactions between and among different parts of the system (i.e.,
social, environmental, economic, physical, political, and cultural considerations), feedback
loops, unintended consequences, and cross-sector dependencies, and how interactions impact design
and operational decision-making.

**ST-C3** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 4, 10, 13, 16, 17
Apply relevant concepts and frameworks from across disciplines to the study of real-world problems
and their solutions, with ethical discernment and empathetic consideration for uncertainties;
communities/societies, environmental, energy, and climate justice; and cultural awareness.

**ST-C4** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 6, 7, 9, 11, 12-15
Create solutions that are viable, inclusive, and equitable and that consider the scale of the
activity/product/infrastructure relative to local ecosystems and planetary boundaries (i.e.,
carrying capacities, tipping points, environmental and social thresholds and allocations);
consider the potential roles of bio-inspired, nature-based, and regenerative solutions.

**ST-C5** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 9, 11-15
Create designs that include communities/societies, environmental ecosystems, and the diversity of
life they sustain while keeping systems dynamics concepts in mind (e.g., feedback loops, complex
cause-effect chains, cascading effects, inertia, tipping points, legacy, resilience, adaptation,
energy systems and flows).

## Supplemental

**ST-S1** | Low | ABET: 2 | SDG: 4, 9, 11-13
Explain key concepts in system dynamics (e.g., upstream and end-of-pipe factors), the impacts of
system dynamics on communities/societies and design solutions, and how system dynamics can help
to improve design solutions.

**ST-S2** | High | ABET: 2, 4, 5 | SDG: 3, 9-11, 13, 16
Design safe, just, and context-appropriate solutions for dynamic, real-world problems in
partnership with communities using human- and life-centered design and system dynamics, including
feedback loops, tipping points, and system resilience.

**ST-S3** | High | ABET: 6, 7 | SDG: 4, 9, 11-13, 17
Create visual system maps (e.g., causal loop diagramming, system dynamics simulations).

**ST-S4** | High | ABET: 1, 4 | SDG: 4, 8, 9, 11-13
Critique common system archetypes (e.g., limits of growth, drifting goals, tragedy of the
commons).

# Environmental Literacy

Knowledge and Understanding.

## Core

**EL-C1** | Medium | ABET: 4, 7 | SDG: 1-17
Demonstrate sustainability literacy: understand the interconnectedness of environmental, social,
and economic systems; understand various definitions and historical contexts of environmental,
social, and economic sustainability (e.g., UN Sustainable Development Goals, Brundtland report,
Triple Bottom Line, Four Pillars of Sustainable Development, Earth Charter), greenwashing,
greenhushing; understand the triple crises of equity, climate, and nature; think critically and
solve problems; promote sustainable practices; and participate actively in creating a safe,
sustainable, resilient, and equitable world.

**EL-C2** | Low | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 4, 9, 11-13
Articulate whole life cycle and closed-loop systems thinking as related to the impact of their
work (e.g., understanding of life cycle burdens of design alternatives).

**EL-C3** | Medium | ABET: 1, 2 | SDG: 6, 9, 13-15
Examine key global ecosystem cycles and services and Nature's Contributions to People (e.g.,
water, carbon, energy, and nitrogen/phosphorus cycles, as well as nutrient cycling, soil
formation, pollination, waste decomposition), how they are interconnected, their relationship to
nature, biodiversity, and climate change, and how they impact design solutions.

**EL-C4** | Medium | ABET: 1, 2, 6 | SDG: 7, 9, 11-13
Demonstrate energy literacy: understand the nature and role of energy sources (primary vs.
secondary) and energy data (direct and indirect); how energy is used (embodied vs. operational);
how electrical circuits work and are used (e.g., voltage, regulation, grids, microgrids); how
efficiently energy is converted and stored (e.g., batteries); and how energy effectiveness
strategies and energy-use decisions affect renewable use, carbon footprint, and overall energy
used throughout the solution life cycle.

**EL-C5** | Medium | ABET: 4, 6 | SDG: 4, 12-17
Demonstrate data literacy: understand, assess, critique, verify, and effectively utilize data and
reporting (e.g., UN Global Compact, Global Reporting Initiative) about environmental and social
issues (e.g., biodiversity loss; climate change, carbon accounting, and greenhouse gas emissions;
energy and water use; pollution and toxicity; **air quality**; waste management) including
consideration for past/current/future and local/regional/global impacts and without greenwashing.

## Supplemental

**EL-S1** | Low | ABET: 1, 2 | SDG: 2, 6, 12, 14, 15
Explain abiotic assets (e.g., fossil fuels, minerals, metals), flows (e.g., wind and solar energy,
water, air), and biotic natural capital (e.g., ecosystems, biodiversity), and the function of key
ecosystem services including provisioning services (e.g., agriculture/food, water), regulating and
maintenance services, cultural services, and supporting services (e.g., material cycles, energy
cycles).

**EL-S2** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 12-17
Apply environmental laws, ethics, and policies (i.e., frameworks, regulations, legislations) at
regional, national, and global levels, and consider ethical, social, environmental, energy, and
climate justice, and cultural implications **beyond current environmental compliance** and
geopolitical boundaries.

**EL-S3** | Medium | ABET: 1, 4, 6 | SDG: 7, 11-13
Demonstrate climate literacy: understand the essential principles of Earth's climate system and
that the climate crisis is urgent, global, complex, and caused by human activity primarily through
the burning of fossil fuels; understand and know the differences between weather, climate, and
direct/indirect causes of climate change; understand scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions; recognize
credible information about climate change, know where to find it and how to use it for resilience
planning and to create climate mitigation and adaptation solutions; communicate about climate
change and climate data in accurate and effective ways; and make informed decisions related to
climate change.

# Responsible Business and Economy

Knowledge and Understanding.

## Core

**RBE-C1** | Low | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7-13
Recognize opportunities and demand for circular, inclusive, and sustainable business models, such
as models that leverage product durability (e.g., renting, upgradeability, repairability,
modularity, resale) and reduce energy and water usage, protect consumers and their privacy,
reflect and include the interests and needs of diverse users and consumers, and reflect and
include ethical, safety, and justice considerations.

**RBE-C2** | Medium | ABET: 2, 7 | SDG: 7-9, 11-13, 17
Examine risks and opportunities related to changing social, economic, political, ecological,
energy, and climate systems on their work (e.g., extended costs, value, trade-offs, partnerships,
regulations, policies, energy hotspots), exploring approaches to change from inside and outside of
their experience, discipline, region, and country.

**RBE-C3** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7-13, 17
Demonstrate awareness of how different revenue and business models can positively or negatively
influence environmental and social systems (e.g., circular economy models, shared ownership
models, service models, leasing with take-back instead of asset sales for planned obsolescence;
employee-owned, public-private partnerships, business-NGO collaboration models).

**RBE-C4** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 8-15, 17
Demonstrate awareness of alternative forms of capital beyond financial resources (including
natural/ecological, human, social, and physical resources) and their limits, and demonstrate
awareness of emerging economic systems intended to promote environmental, social, and global
responsibility in economic thinking (e.g., Doughnut Economics, circular economy).

**RBE-C5** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 8, 9, 11-15
Weigh the short- and long-term social and environmental costs and value of their work.

## Supplemental

**RBE-S1** | Low | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 1, 5, 7-13, 17
Explain alternative business, revenue, and entrepreneurship models (e.g., circular economy,
B Corps, product service systems, sharing economy platforms, cooperatives, indigenous
practices/sensibilities).

**RBE-S2** | Low | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15
Explain sustainable, circular, restorative, and regenerative practices to consumers.

**RBE-S3** | Low | ABET: 2, 5 | SDG: 6-11, 13, 17
Locate appropriate funding sources and approaches for public infrastructure by working with local
communities.

**RBE-S4** | Medium | ABET: 4, 6 | SDG: 3, 8, 9, 12, 13, 16, 17
Apply management and measurement systems (e.g., Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS), Global
Reporting Initiative (GRI), International Organization for Standardization (ISO)) as tools to
enable systematic integration of sustainability impact management into business practices.

**RBE-S5** | High | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 8-10, 12, 13, 16, 17
Judge supply chain agents, vendors, etc., from environmental, social, equity, diversity,
inclusion, and justice perspectives.

**RBE-S6** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7-13
Compare economic trade-offs in sustainability efforts (e.g., between energy efficiency,
performance, and cost) and how financial trade-offs may apply differently to a variety of people
within each unique value chain.

**RBE-S7** | High | ABET: 2, 4, 7 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15, 17
Evaluate financial incentives for sustainable engineering, including carbon pricing, green
investment funds, regulatory compliance, and ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) criteria.

# Social Responsibility

Knowledge and Understanding.

## Core

**SR-C1** | Low | ABET: 4, 7 | SDG: 1-17
Understand the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals as a framework for advancing social
sustainability solutions and initiatives as a globally responsible designer/engineer.

**SR-C2** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 3, 5, 10, 11, 13, 16
Recognize and empathize with ethical, climate, environmental, and social justice implications
relative to social and cultural impacts of their work.

**SR-C3** | Medium | ABET: 4 | SDG: 1, 3, 5, 8-12, 16
Analyze how engineering activities directly and indirectly cause positive and negative social and
cultural impacts throughout the design life cycle, both to workers producing the products (e.g.,
labor practices, livelihood, health) and to communities, society, and non-human life (e.g.,
resources acquisition, waste production and management, traditional/cultural methodologies).

**SR-C4** | Medium | ABET: 4 | SDG: 1, 3, 5, 10, 11, 13, 16
Examine the ways in which some communities and populations (e.g., low income, children, elderly,
women, people with disabilities, people of color) have historically been and continue to be
negatively impacted and/or intentionally marginalized and underserved and continue to be
disproportionately negatively impacted by engineering activities.

**SR-C5** | High | ABET: 4 | SDG: 3, 5-7, 10-13, 16
Evaluate the role of social responsibility and environmental, energy, and climate justice in the
engineering profession (e.g., policies, laws, social justice, public health, national security).

**SR-C6** | High | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 8, 10-12, 16
Assess cultural, local, and global implications and influences in the context of their work (e.g.,
cultural expressions and sensitivities, services and goods procurement, heritage site
appreciation, Indigenous Sovereignty) as well as equity awareness (e.g., gender, sex, race,
ethnicity, sexual orientation, socioeconomic class).

**SR-C7** | High | ABET: 2, 5 | SDG: 8-11, 16, 17
Create robust, dynamic, and resilient social systems and transdisciplinary networks.

## Supplemental

**SR-S1** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 1, 3, 5, 10, 13-17
Recognize the breadth of social and environmental justice issues, indigenous rights, laws,
policies, and commitments (e.g., Global Compact).

**SR-S2** | Low | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 1, 3, 6-8, 10-15
Recognize social and cultural implications related to local, regional, and global materials and
energy use (e.g., land changes, surface and groundwater use and pollution, **air pollution**,
energy production and use, toxins, labor rights, land tenure) as a global citizen.

**SR-S3** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 1, 3, 10, 11, 13, 16
Recognize how climate change impacts are disproportionately borne by low-income, marginalized,
underserved, and vulnerable groups (e.g., children, elderly, women, people with disabilities,
people of color) due to historical and present-day disparities that reduce their capacity to
prepare for, recover from, and adapt to climate impacts, and that these populations often live in
the poorest regions of the world that emit less carbon dioxide.

# Environmental Impact Assessment

Skills, Experiences and Behaviors: Technical Skills.

## Core

**EIA-C1** | Low | ABET: 2, 6 | SDG: 6, 7, 9, 11-15
Explain high-level environmental impact assessments (e.g., Life Cycle Assessments, carbon
accounting, energy impact assessments, and life cycle hazards), how they work, their scope, what
information they require, how to incorporate their findings, and why they matter.

**EIA-C2** | Low | ABET: 2, 7 | SDG: 7, 9, 11-13
Recognize current reporting standards, eco-labelling systems, and certificates (e.g., EPEAT,
Energy Star, LEED), including outside their geography, that could be used to improve their work.

**EIA-C3** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4, 6 | SDG: 3, 7-9, 11-15
Interpret assessment metrics and broader environmental, social, and economic implications of their
work.

**EIA-C4** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 8-13, 16, 17
Examine complex or contradictory information to explain, prioritize, and make decisions among
trade-offs (e.g., What are the cost, performance, and resource implications of the decision? Have
relevant people been included in the process? Are marginalized and underserved communities part of
the process and decision-making? Who and what will be most impacted by the decision?).

## Supplemental

**EIA-S1** | Low | ABET: 2, 6 | SDG: 7, 9, 11-13
Discuss relative impact reduction vs. absolute impact reduction (e.g., reduction in greenhouse
gases emitted by a vehicle per mile vs. reduction of GHGs emitted annually by the factory
producing the vehicles).

**EIA-S2** | High | ABET: 4, 6 | SDG: 3, 6, 8, 9, 12-16
Assess Environmental, Health, and Safety standards (e.g., chemical hazard assessments, how to
research chemical safety) data, and specifications for inputs, outputs, and performance levels of
engineered products and services.

**EIA-S3** | High | ABET: 2, 6 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15, 17
Evaluate Life Cycle Assessment, Techno-Economic Assessment, energy assessments, or other
assessment tool outcomes at various scales of length (local and global effects), time (acute and
chronic effects), and impacts (second and third order impacts, time-delayed impacts).

# Materials

Skills, Experiences and Behaviors: Technical Skills.

## Core

**MAT-C1** | Low | ABET: 1, 2 | SDG: 6, 8, 9, 11-15
Identify potential impacts of materials and materials design (e.g., embodied and operational
energy, energy demand, greenhouse gas emissions, toxicity, natural resource use, labor impacts)
through materials screening and selection process throughout the supply chain and life cycle, from
raw material extraction through manufacturing, use, reuse/recycling, and end of life, with a focus
on making design and operational decisions to minimize negative impacts to nature and all life.

**MAT-C2** | Low | ABET: 7 | SDG: 9, 11-15
Recognize current environmental assessment research and gaps in data and research for materials,
materials screening and selection, materials design, and materials systems.

**MAT-C3** | High | ABET: 1, 4 | SDG: 8-15, 17
Critique the environmental and social impacts and audits of design-appropriate materials (e.g.,
technical considerations including strength, weight, cost, toxicity, extraction impacts, material
compatibility, thermal and structural properties) as part of the screening and selection process
with a sustainability ethos, including materials, systems, and processes to make decisions about
whether to use existing or design new materials.

**MAT-C4** | Medium | ABET: 1, 2 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15
Compare materials properties (e.g., chemical, physical, and structural properties using
thermodynamics, kinetics, the interrelated materials tetrahedron) aligned with sustainable design
end-use application.

**MAT-C5** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 4, 7-9, 11-15
Demonstrate sustainable design literacy: select materials for design alternatives and trade-offs
that enable a long functional lifetime, promote circularity, support nature-based and regenerative
solutions, reduce energy consumption and reduce/prevent hotspots, have net zero greenhouse gas
emissions impact, cause minimal or no environmental and social harm, and/or are restorative to
social, cultural, and environmental systems.

**MAT-C6** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7, 9, 11-16
Design with materials to create product and infrastructure solutions that are regenerative and/or
reduce negative social and environmental impacts (e.g., consider natural materials) with an
aligned understanding of traditional industrial materials (e.g., iron, steel, aluminum) and
innovations that reduce fossil fuel consumption (e.g., recycled steel, green concrete).

## Supplemental

**MAT-S1** | Medium | ABET: 6, 7 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15, 17
Implement tools, resources, and data assessments (e.g., AI, Open LCA, Sustainability Computer
Simulation, process simulation, circularity metrics, digital twins, Ashby plots, footprint tools,
Integration Computational Manufacturing Engineering, materials databases, Material Input per Unit
of Service) to identify, screen, document, and mitigate potential social and environmental impacts
of materials/materials design/supply chain throughout the entire life cycle, from raw material
extraction through transportation, processing, manufacturing, use, reuse/recycling, and end of
life, with a zero waste, circular, regenerative, and/or restorative perspective.

**MAT-S2** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 8, 9, 11-15, 17
Examine the implications of material consumption at scale, including how material selection and
supply chain can influence the scale of positive or negative impacts on sustainable design.

**MAT-S3** | Medium | ABET: 4, 7 | SDG: 4, 7-9, 11-15
Demonstrate materials literacy: understand materials origins, properties, and life cycle;
understand materials composition at macro- and micro-materials levels; be aware that macro
materials include those with structural properties (e.g., concrete, metals, plastics) and
functional properties (e.g., chemicals and solid/liquid/intermediary states); be aware that
substances of concern can be bound up in engineered materials, composites, and products (often
micro materials, chemicals, and nanoparticles).

**MAT-S4** | Medium | ABET: 7 | SDG: 8, 9, 12-15, 17
Analyze innovation gaps in existing materials options and identify ways to spur appropriate
research and development.

**MAT-S5** | Medium | ABET: 2, 6 | SDG: 7, 9, 11-13
Apply a systems perspective and calculate the embodied and operational energy of materials to make
informed decisions.

**MAT-S6** | High | ABET: 4, 6 | SDG: 3, 6, 8, 9, 12-16
Evaluate Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) standards, assessments, laws (e.g., Safety Data
Sheets, Globally Harmonized System, OSHA compliance, Restriction of Hazardous Substances, Waste
from Electrical and Electronic Equipment, REACH regulation, ecotoxicity, chemical hazard
assessments), and green chemistry aspects of materials.

**MAT-S7** | High | ABET: 4, 7 | SDG: 7-10, 12, 13, 16, 17
Balance trade-offs of using AI (large computational models) to explore new materials and
innovation gaps with AI's environmental and social implications (e.g., energy and water use,
location of data storage infrastructure, ethics of AI data sourcing).

# Design

Skills, Experiences and Behaviors: Technical Skills.

## Core

**DES-C1** | Medium | ABET: 2, 6 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15
Execute technical analyses (e.g., Life Cycle Assessment, Techno-Economic Assessment) to choose
strategies that maximize positive impacts and minimize negative environmental and social impacts
to achieve design and operational goals.

**DES-C2** | High | ABET: 2 | SDG: 7-9, 11-15
Design, including materials design, using a systems thinking approach for the environment and
society based on discipline-specific technical skills and circular design strategies (e.g.,
light-weighting, repairability, durability, upgradeability, disassembly, reuse/recycling,
flexibility, resiliency, extreme affordability, for part or whole recovery to eliminate waste).

**DES-C3** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7, 9, 11-17
Create long-term, systems thinking-based approaches for tackling urgent challenges (e.g., climate
mitigation and adaptation), preventing negative environmental and/or social impacts (e.g.,
reduction in greenhouse gas emissions, reduction in energy and resource use, greater resilience),
including use of innovative and disruptive solutions within supply chains, design of new
technologies and materials, use of nature-based solutions.

## Supplemental

**DES-S1** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 1, 5, 8, 10-15, 17
Recognize local craft traditions, indigenous knowledge systems, and vernacular practices, and
innovate viable, inclusive, equitable, and regenerative solutions and processes.

**DES-S2** | Medium | ABET: 3, 4 | SDG: 3, 5, 10-13, 16
Implement user experience/participatory studies (e.g., design thinking, human-centered design,
community-based projects), social impact assessments, and persuasive design strategies to
cultivate sustainable behavior and to meet user/community needs in responsible, novel, improved,
ethical, resilient, long-lasting, and sustainable ways.

**DES-S3** | High | ABET: 2, 6 | SDG: 7-9, 11-13
Design using rapid prototyping and iteration with approaches that incorporate whole life cycle,
systems thinking, and systems dynamics concepts in mind (e.g., feedback loops, complex
cause-effect chains, cascading effects, inertia, tipping points, legacy, resilience, adaptation).

**DES-S4** | High | ABET: 3, 5 | SDG: 5, 8-13, 16, 17
Develop creative transdisciplinary, culturally-appropriate ideas and solutions in engineering
contexts, along with social and cultural values by working across disciplines.

**DES-S5** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7-9, 11-17
Create solutions for use with alternative economic models (e.g., circular economy, Doughnut
Economics, sharing economy).

**DES-S6** | High | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 9, 11, 13-15, 17
Design infrastructure and products that account for increasing climate risk, variability, and
vulnerabilities due to extreme events (e.g., extreme heat, flooding, hurricanes, sea level rise)
to enhance strategies for adaptation and build resilient infrastructures, systems, and
communities.

# Critical Thinking

Skills, Experiences and Behaviors: Leadership Skills.

## Core

**CT-C1** | Low | ABET: 1, 4 | SDG: 3, 8-15
Define problems comprehensively with consideration of uncertainties and long-term environmental
and social consequences, both intended and unintended.

**CT-C2** | Medium | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 10, 11, 13, 16
Practice being self-reflective, valuing and guarding the environment, being empathetic, bringing
an inclusive mindset, and cultivating intercultural competence to advance equitable and effective
change for all life.

**CT-C3** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 4, 9, 11-13, 16
Understand that their values are both shaping and being shaped by the designs, technologies, and
innovations they create, implement, and scale.

**CT-C4** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 4, 11-13
Recognize that every person has a role in sustainability and climate resilience planning and
action, and has the right and need to be informed about the environmental/social/economic impacts
of the products they purchase, consume, and discard, including the understanding of fossil fuels
as the main cause of climate change.

**CT-C5** | Medium | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 10-13, 16
Examine social norms, cultural values, and implicit biases that underlie their behaviors (e.g.,
normative thinking, cognitive dissonance) and how these influence ethical and equitable
decision-making and adaptive responses to sustainability challenges, including climate change.

**CT-C6** | High | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 10-13, 16
Critique complex ethical, cultural, and values-based thinking and choices, employing empathy and
considering justice when evaluating conflicts of interest, trade-offs, timescales, and uncertain
knowledge within problem constraints.

## Supplemental

**CT-S1** | Low | ABET: 3, 4 | SDG: 5, 10, 11, 16
Discuss varying standpoints with integrity, authenticity, and empathy for different perspectives,
opinions, views, cultures, and lived experiences (i.e., normative thinking).

**CT-S2** | Low | ABET: 4 | SDG: 7, 11-13
Identify issues and actions of environmental and social priority to respond to urgent challenges,
such as dependency on fossil fuels and the climate crisis.

**CT-S3** | Medium | ABET: 4, 6 | SDG: 4, 10-13, 16, 17
Implement sustainability-related qualitative and quantitative research into ethical and
equity-oriented decision-making processes.

**CT-S4** | Medium | ABET: 4 | SDG: 3, 11-16
Distinguish the consequences of their actions and how to deal with risks and changes (i.e., apply
the precautionary principle).

**CT-S5** | Medium | ABET: 2, 4 | SDG: 7-13, 16
Compare the pros, cons, and trade-offs of incremental versus radical innovations to foster ethical
and equitable discernment and decisions/solutions that reduce dependency on fossil fuels and
prioritize environmental and social well-being over short-term economic gains.

**CT-S6** | Medium | ABET: 4, 6, 7 | SDG: 4, 7-10, 12, 13, 16, 17
Demonstrate Artificial Intelligence (AI) literacy: understand the social, environmental, and
global impacts and responsible use of digital infrastructure and AI technology (e.g., cloud
computing, energy and water use, data storage, Generative AI and Large Language Models,
Transformers, Reinforcement Learning, and other AI-driven systems) and critically assess
AI-generated data and outputs for accuracy and credibility (detect misinformation) to be informed,
adaptable, and ethically aware participants in an AI-enabled future.

**CT-S7** | Medium | ABET: 7 | SDG: 4, 8, 9, 11-13, 16
Demonstrate motivation, curiosity, and creative and critical thinking to support resilient,
flexible, and agile thinking and behaviors.

**CT-S8** | High | ABET: 2, 5 | SDG: 3, 8, 9, 11-17
Prioritize appropriate and sustainable designs and solutions based on the context of the problem
and in collaboration with experts and the people, lives, and ecosystems that impact and are
impacted by the designs and solutions.

**CT-S9** | High | ABET: 4, 7 | SDG: 4, 8-13, 16, 17
Evaluate possible, probable, and desirable futures for diverse people, societies, and cultures, to
create their own visions for the future (i.e., futures literacy).

# Communication and Teamwork

Skills, Experiences and Behaviors: Leadership Skills.

## Core

**COM-C1** | Medium | ABET: 3 | SDG: 4, 5, 10-13, 16, 17
Communicate using culturally appropriate and audience-specific written, graphic/visual, oral,
storytelling, and/or interpersonal communication skills (e.g., clear, concise, calls to action,
key takeaways) to define the problem, convey technical ideas to experts, non-experts, and
decision-makers, and to negotiate, persuade, influence, and/or advocate for effective innovations
and known solutions, principles, values, goals, and targets.

**COM-C2** | High | ABET: 4, 5 | SDG: 3-5, 10, 11, 13, 16
Develop a culturally-sensitive and resilient identity (e.g., values, beliefs, morals, interests,
self-efficacy, emotional intelligence, empathy, humility, courage, agency, tenacity, optimism,
ethics-based personal code of conduct, navigating personal moral injury and dilemmas,
self-learning) that supports wellbeing, ecological belonging, and creative leadership potential to
enable and advance positive change through inspirational sustainability visioning, persuasion, and
advocacy.

**COM-C3** | Medium | ABET: 5 | SDG: 4, 5, 8-13, 17
Demonstrate ability to work within, empower, and function well on teams and across disciplines
(e.g., actively engage, ask questions, listen with a willingness to process and learn, navigate
tensions and complexity constructively, build coalitions, change roles) to advance sustainability
initiatives.

**COM-C4** | Medium | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 10, 11, 13, 16
Demonstrate self-awareness and understanding of unconscious bias that may impact environmental and
climate equity and justice.

**COM-C5** | High | ABET: 5 | SDG: 5, 8, 10-12, 16
Prioritize projects, schedules, and time, and manage people justly, equitably, and inclusively.

**COM-C6** | High | ABET: 3, 4, 5 | SDG: 3, 4, 8, 9, 11-16
Champion sustainability-focused values and approaches to maintain the integrity of design criteria
across environmental, ecological, and human dimensions.

## Supplemental

**COM-S1** | Low | ABET: 3 | SDG: 4, 11-13, 16, 17
Explain technical and engineering concepts, assumptions, and evidence (e.g., Life Cycle Assessment
and Techno-Economic Assessment outcomes) in lay terms to the public, government, and
clients/customers to influence understanding and acceptance of environmental, social, and cultural
considerations, impacts, and decision-making.

**COM-S2** | High | ABET: 3, 5 | SDG: 4, 5, 8, 10-13, 16, 17
Build lasting and respectful relationships and networks (i.e., through strong inter- and
intrapersonal skills) to advance sustainability efforts.

**COM-S3** | High | ABET: 4 | SDG: 5, 10, 11, 16
Investigate solutions to individual, institutional, and systemic implicit bias.

**COM-S4** | Medium | ABET: 5 | SDG: 8, 9, 11-13, 17
Apply systematic, disciplined, and collaborative project management methodologies in order to
effectively manage teams and themselves.

**COM-S5** | Medium | ABET: 5 | SDG: 4, 5, 8-13, 17
Demonstrate ability to relate and adapt to, interact with, collaborate on, and lead
multidisciplinary teams, effectively representing an engineering perspective in a comprehensible
manner through real-world, hands-on, project-based work.
