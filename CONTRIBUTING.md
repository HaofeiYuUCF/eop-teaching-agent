# Contribute and use teaching resources

You can contribute teaching knowledge in ordinary English. You do not need to write code, edit
metadata, or use Git to prepare a contribution. Supply material and corrections to the assistant;
the assistant prepares readable drafts. Repository maintainers can handle file naming and publication.
These instructions have not yet been evaluated with faculty without programming experience.

## Choose what you want to do

| Your purpose | What to supply | What you receive |
|---|---|---|
| Find a reference | A topic or question | Relevant source passages and page locations; start with the [library](library/index.md). |
| Contribute a topic card | Slides, notes, an explanation, or corrections to an existing card | A draft using the [card template](reference/card-template.md), with sources and questions for you. |
| Contribute a scenario | A decision or teaching question, available reports/data, and intended teaching use | A draft using the [scenario template](reference/scenario-template.md), with evidence and limits. |
| Adapt your teaching | Your material, learning goals, student preparation, and available time/tools | An Intake conversation and lesson plan for review before assessment and material generation. |
| Report experience after use | The resource/version used and what actually happened | An optional [use and revision record](reference/faculty-use-record.md) to guide corrections. |

Reference lookup and resource contribution do not require designing or generating a lesson.
An instructor may use a resource without contributing one, or contribute without teaching with it.

## Materials, cards, and scenarios

Source materials retain the original content and its provenance. A topic card organizes reusable
teaching knowledge: objectives, prerequisites, difficulties, adaptations, technical content, evidence,
EOP connections, and gaps. A scenario provides a decision context that can connect several topics.
One scenario can use several cards, and one card can support different scenarios or a lesson without one.

The current [case catalog](scenario/catalog.md) contains the hypothetical I-4/SR 408 case and the
Sunshine Corridor case based on a dated planning report. Both have documented limitations. You may
select either, provide another context, or use no scenario. Intake recommends a case only when
useful for your objectives and resources; you can override that recommendation in the plan review.

## Preparing a contribution

1. Explain the teaching purpose and provide whatever sources or observations you have. Identify
   the course context and distinguish what you observed from what you expect students might do.
2. The Card Builder prepares or revises a card or scenario. It preserves source locations,
   authorship, differing course treatments, and unresolved questions. It asks only for missing
   information that affects the contribution. A missing field can remain unknown.
3. Inspect the draft and correct its interpretation of your material. The Reviewer checks the
   resource and records the scope of that check. Your acceptance and scientific verification are
   separate: neither an author name nor an AI review implies your endorsement.
4. Keep the revised resource's identifier and revision visible. A new scenario gets a catalog entry
   describing fit and data readiness. A draft can remain partial; its limits travel with later use.
   Preparing a contribution does not by itself publish it or grant reuse rights for supplied assets.

Scenario contributions can draw on faculty knowledge or reports/data supplied by practitioners.
Record who suggested a case, who supplied evidence, and who reviewed the teaching adaptation as
distinct contributions, only when known. A recommendation does not imply approval of the resulting
lesson or a commitment to participate. Label hypothetical cases and exercise inputs explicitly.

## Example requests

- "Turn these slides into a draft card. Preserve my examples and mark inferred student difficulties."
- "Help me draft a scenario from this report. Identify missing data; do not generate a lesson yet."
- "Adapt my lecture without a scenario. Use my learning objectives and available class time."
- "Recommend a case for this class and explain its fit. I want to review the plan first."
- "Record these corrections to the scenario I used, retaining the earlier revision and its sources."

After classroom use, describe the actual context and changes needed. Do not infer student learning
from generated materials or from a favorable instructor impression. Keep private student information
out of public contributions. Formal research data collection is a separate activity, not a prerequisite
for using or improving this prototype.
