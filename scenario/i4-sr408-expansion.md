# Optional teaching scenario

This is a hypothetical project, not a description or prediction of an actual proposal. It can be
used briefly to illustrate concepts or as a complete project exercise, according to the lecture plan.
Use another setting when requested or better suited to the audience.

Read [shared policy](../reference/workflow-policy.md) and the [fact register](../reference/fact-register.md).
Claim IDs below identify assumptions, inherited inputs, and unresolved questions.

## Question and boundaries

HYP-01 proposes a capacity change near I-4/SR 408 and an arterial intersection modification. Ask
what data and assumptions would be needed to evaluate its consequences. Compare activity and
emission-factor changes, model choice, concentration interpretation, and decision criteria at the
depth selected in the plan. Do not prescribe the sign or policy conclusion in advance.

Changing speed and volume can be used to explore different results under explicitly supplied
factors. EF-01/02 are candidate literature examples from a particular fleet; they do not establish
the direction or magnitude for this project. If local evidence is unavailable, select consistent
synthetic factors under HYP-03 and label both inputs and conclusions as hypothetical. Do not claim
a model produced them. Non-exhaust PM extensions require EF-03 evidence or a clearly stipulated
relationship appropriate to the learning objective.

If an EOP systems-thinking objective concerns feedback, include an actual feedback mechanism and
ask students to reason about it. Multiplying two changing inputs alone does not establish feedback.
Traffic growth, induced demand, rerouting, and fleet changes are questions or stipulated scenarios,
not assumed empirical findings about this location.

For an ozone extension, ask what chemical/transport processes the chosen model represents and what
evidence would be needed to infer local versus regional response. Do not require opposite signs at
two scales without supporting conditions. Keep model capability claims tied to the specific model.

## Representative geometry

HYP-02 adopts the following source-demonstration description as hypothetical geometry. It is not a
survey of this interchange or a complete runnable model dataset. Identify any additional coordinates,
traffic, meteorology, model settings, and receptors before claiming an executed model run.

Taken from the ENV 6106 project-level modeling demonstration. Used as a representative geometry
for the hypothetical arterial intersection, not as a claim about any specific real location.

**Link convention.** For through movements, the first 100 m from the intersection are modeled as
queue links; the next 330 m beyond that are running links. Turn lanes are modeled at their stated
storage lengths.

Four approaches, 27 links. Approach widths of approximately 28 m and 35 m on the two axes.

| Link | Movement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Left turn, 150 m |
| 2-4 | Three through lanes |
| 5 | Right turn, 110 m |
| 6-9 | Four through lanes |
| 10 | Left turn, 30 m |
| 11 | Left turn, 50 m |
| 12 | Through and right turn |
| 13 | Right turn, 200 m |
| 14 | Through lane |
| 15 | Left turn, treated as through |
| 16 | Left turn, 90 m |
| 17-18 | Through lanes |
| 19 | Right turn, 100 m |
| 20-22 | Through lanes |
| 23 | Right turn, 140 m |
| 24 | Right turn, 120 m |
| 25-27 | Through lanes |

Acceleration lanes are not accounted for, consistent with the source demonstration. Say so in any
material that uses this geometry; it is a modeling simplification students should see named.

## Audience variants

- Planning: interpret supplied hypothetical results, identify missing evidence, compare alternatives.
- Undergraduate engineering: perform a supported calculation and test an assumption or input change.
- Graduate modeling: justify a model and configuration, execute an appropriately scoped exercise,
  and evaluate sensitivity, assumptions, and uncertainty using applicable data or a labeled benchmark.

These are possibilities, not assignments by major. Select tasks from actual prerequisites and goals.
For real-data work, essential inputs and model capability claims must pass the evidence checks.

## Source gaps and local context

The source package named ENV 6106 project-level modeling materials, ENV 4120 materials, Florida DEP,
FDOT/CO Florida, and FHWA guidance, but it did not include complete runnable source datasets or all
source locators. Keep that limitation visible in the plan and register.

The source scenario deferred researching nearby community history and demographics. Version 3
retains that research gap; it is not a new instruction from the current user. If such an extension
is requested, obtain appropriate evidence and represent communities accurately before teaching local
claims. Do not claim a social-responsibility outcome without the corresponding evidence/activity.
